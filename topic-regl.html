Real Estate, Gaming & Lodging (REGL)

Real Estate, Gaming, and Lodging (REGL) is a specialized coverage universe that requires distinct valuation methodologies and metric analysis compared to standard corporate finance. While standard companies are valued on cash earnings (EBITDA), REGL companies are often valued on asset values (NAV), cash distributions (FFO/AFFO), or yield-based frameworks. Understanding this distinction is fundamental to working in this group.

Basic — The REGL Landscape & Property Types

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

  • Equity REITs: Own and manage properties. This is the primary focus of most banking coverage.
  • Mortgage REITs (mREITs): Invest in mortgages and mortgage-backed securities (often covered by FIG teams).

Primary Property Types

  • Multifamily (Residential): Apartment buildings. Lease Term: Short (1 year). Profile: Considered "defensive" or "safe" because people always need shelter. Rents can mark-to-market quickly due to short lease terms.
  • Office: Skyscrapers and suburban office parks. Lease Term: Long (5-15 years). Profile: Cyclical. High Capital Expenditures (Tenant Improvements) are required to attract new tenants. Vacancy is a major risk.
  • Industrial: Warehouses, logistics centers, and distribution hubs. Lease Term: Medium to Long. Profile: Currently high growth due to e-commerce.
  • Retail: Malls (Enclosed) and Strip Centers (Open-air). Lease Term: Long (5-10 years). Profile: Highly sensitive to consumer spending and tenant credit health.
  • Self-Storage: Facilities renting small units to individuals. Lease Term: Monthly. Profile: Extremely resilient. In downturns, people downsize and store goods. In upturns, people buy more stuff. Low Capital Intensity.

Business Models & Structures

The REIT Structure

  • Asset Test: At least 75% of total assets must be real estate, cash, or treasuries.
  • Income Test: At least 75% of gross income must come from real estate sources (rents, mortgage interest).
  • Distribution: Must pay out at least 90% of taxable income to shareholders as dividends.
💡 Key Insight: Because REITs distribute 90% of their income, they retain very little earnings to fund growth. Therefore, REITs are constantly dependent on the capital markets (issuing equity and debt) to finance acquisitions and development. This means the cost of capital is critical to their business model — a REIT cannot grow if its stock is trading at a discount to NAV, making it "dilutive" to issue new shares.

Lease Structures: Triple-Net (NNN) vs. Gross

  • Gross Lease: The landlord (REIT) pays all property expenses (taxes, insurance, maintenance). The tenant pays a flat rent. Risk: Landlord bears the risk of rising utility or tax costs.
  • Triple-Net Lease (NNN): The tenant pays the rent plus all property expenses (Property Taxes, Insurance, Maintenance). Mnemonic: N-N-N stands for Net of Taxes, Net of Insurance, Net of Maintenance. Risk: Landlord has very stable, bond-like cash flows. Very popular with institutional investors seeking income.

The Property Pro-Forma

  1. Gross Potential Rent (GPR): The total revenue if the building were 100% occupied at market rates.
  2. Effective Gross Income (EGI): Start with GPR and adjust for reality: Subtract Vacancy Allowance, Credit Loss, Concessions. Add Ancillary Income.
  3. Net Operating Income (NOI): NOI = EGI — Operating Expenses. Analogy: NOI is the "EBITDA" of the property world.
  4. Adjusted NOI / Cash Flow: Deduct Tenant Improvements (TIs), Leasing Commissions (LCs), and Capital Expenditures (CapEx).

Key Industry Metrics

Earnings Metrics

  • Funds From Operations (FFO): The primary earnings metric for REITs. FFO = Net Income + Depreciation (Real Estate) — Gains/(Losses) on Asset Sales. Why? Real estate usually appreciates, but GAAP depreciation reduces Net Income as if the building were losing value. FFO adds it back to show true cash earning power.
  • Adjusted FFO (AFFO): AFFO = FFO - Recurring Maintenance CapEx — Straight-line Rent Adjustments. Usage: Used to determine the safety of the dividend.
  • Same-Store NOI (SS-NOI): Measures growth of properties owned for the full period. Eliminates the distortion caused by buying or selling assets during the year.

Yield Metrics

  • Cap Rate (Capitalization Rate): The unlevered yield. Cap Rate = NOI / Property Value. Interpretation: A 5% Cap Rate means for every $100 of value, the property generates $5 of NOI. High Cap Rate = Lower Value (higher yield for more risk). Low Cap Rate = Higher Value (lower yield for lower risk).
  • Cash-on-Cash Yield: A levered return metric often used in private equity. Cash-on-Cash = (NOI - Debt Service) / Equity Invested.

Operational Metrics

  • RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Room): For Hotels. ADR × Occupancy.
  • Occupancy Cost Ratio: For Retail. (Rent + CAM) / Tenant Sales. If a tenant pays $10k in rent and sells $100k of goods, the ratio is 10%. If this ratio gets too high (e.g., >15-20%), the tenant may go bankrupt, creating vacancy risk for the REIT.

Valuation Methodologies

Net Asset Value (NAV)

  • Value the Assets: Apply a Cap Rate to the forward NOI of the portfolio. Example: Portfolio NOI is $100M, Market Cap Rate is 5%, Asset Value = $2B.
  • Add Other Assets: Cash, Land Bank, Developments in Progress (at cost or slight premium).
  • Subtract Liabilities: Debt (marked to market), Preferred Stock.
  • Result: Net Asset Value (Equity Value). Compare this to the current Market Cap.

Replacement Cost

  • Calculates how much it would cost to build the exact same building today (Land + Material + Labor).
  • If a REIT is trading at $300/sq. ft. but it costs $500/sq. ft. to build, the REIT is trading at a discount to replacement cost. This implies limited downside risk because no developer will build new competing supply at a loss.

Advanced — NAV Build-Up

  • Operating Portfolio: Calculate the Forward 12-Month Net Operating Income (NOI) for the core portfolio. Divide by the appropriate sector-specific and geography-specific Cap Rate.
  • Development Pipeline: Apply a risk-adjusted margin to the expected completion value.
  • Land Bank: Often valued at book value or market comparables (price per acre/buildable square foot). Land generates no income but holds option value.
  • Mark-to-Market Debt: If a REIT has fixed-rate debt at 3% and current market rates are 6%, the debt is worth less than face value to the lender. This liability reduction increases the NAV for equity holders.
💡 Key Insight: REITs trading at a significant discount to NAV are often takeover targets or candidates for privatization, as a buyer could effectively buy the company for less than the liquidation value of its assets.

Development Valuation: Yield on Cost vs. Market Cap Rate

  • Yield on Cost: Stabilized NOI / Total Development Cost.
  • Spread: Yield on Cost - Market Cap Rate.

If a developer builds an office tower for $100M (Total Cost) that generates $7M in NOI, the Yield on Cost is 7%. If comparable buildings trade at a 5% Cap Rate, the building is worth $140M ($7M / 5%) upon stabilization. The developer created $40M of value ($140M - $100M). This is the reward for taking construction risk.

💡 Interview Tip: In interviews, if asked about development risk, mention that banks typically require a development spread of 150-200 basis points (e.g., 7% yield vs. 5% cap rate) to justify the construction risk and illiquidity premium.

Real Estate Private Equity (REPE) Mechanics

The "Waterfall" (Promote Structure)

  • Return of Capital: 100% of cash goes to LPs and GP pro-rata until they get their initial investment back.
  • Preferred Return (Hurdle 1): Cash flows go pro-rata until investors achieve a specific IRR (e.g., 8%).
  • Catch-Up: The GP gets 100% of available cash until their total profit split matches the target ratio (e.g., 20% of total profits).
  • Carried Interest (Promote): Remaining profits are split with a skew toward the GP (e.g., 80% to LP / 20% to GP, or even 70/30).

M&A Considerations

UPREIT Structure (721 Exchange)

  • Mechanism: The seller contributes their property to the REIT's Operating Partnership in exchange for OP Units instead of cash.
  • Benefit: This contribution is tax-deferred (like a 1031 exchange). The seller only pays taxes when they convert OP Units to common stock or cash later.
  • Strategic Value: UPREITs have a competitive advantage in acquisitions because they offer this tax-efficient currency to family-owned dynasties.

Sale-Leaseback (SLB) Mechanics

  • Arbitrage: Real estate trades at lower yields (higher multiples) than operating businesses.
  • Multiple Arbitrage Example: Casino trades at 8× EBITDA. REIT trades at 14× EBITDA. Casino sells property at 14×. Uses cash to buy back its own stock (trading at 8×). Result: Immediate accretion to EPS and equity value.

Lodging & Gaming Nuances

Lodging: The "Split" Model

  • C-Corp (Brand): Marriott/Hilton. Asset-light. Earns franchise fees. High ROIC, High P/E.
  • REIT (Owner): Host Hotels. Asset-heavy. Owns the building. High CapEx, Low P/E, Valued on NAV.

Gaming: OpCo / PropCo

  • PropCo (REIT like VICI): Owns land/building. Collects Rent. Valuation: Yield/FFO.
  • OpCo (Casino like Caesars): Runs the games. Pays Rent. Valuation: EV/EBITDAR.
  • Coverage Ratio: Investors look at EBITDAR / Rent Coverage. A ratio >2.0x is healthy. It ensures the casino makes enough money to pay the rent 2× over.
