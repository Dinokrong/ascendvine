Valuation Overview & Comparable Companies

There are three core valuation methodologies used in IB: Comparable Company Analysis (Comps), Precedent Transactions, and DCF. Each gives a different perspective on value, and they're used together to build a "football field" valuation range.

The Three Core Methodologies

  • Comparable Company Analysis (Trading Comps): Value based on how similar public companies trade in the market today
  • Precedent Transactions: Value based on prices paid in historical M&A deals for similar companies
  • DCF (Discounted Cash Flow): Intrinsic value based on the present value of projected future cash flows
Precedent transactions typically yield higher values than trading comps because they include a control premium — the premium paid to acquire majority control of a company (typically 20–40%).

Comparable Company Analysis (Comps)

Process

  1. Select a universe of comparable public companies (similar size, sector, geography, business model)
  2. Spread key financial metrics: Revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, Net Income (LTM and forward estimates)
  3. Calculate EV and Equity Value multiples: EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue, P/E
  4. Apply the median/mean multiples to the subject company's financials
  5. Derive an implied valuation range

Common Multiples

  • EV/EBITDA: Most commonly used; capital-structure-neutral
  • EV/Revenue: Used when EBITDA is negative or inconsistent (high-growth tech, early-stage)
  • P/E: Useful for financial companies and mature businesses with stable earnings
  • EV/EBIT: Useful when capex intensity differs significantly across peers

Precedent Transactions

Similar methodology to comps, but uses announced/closed M&A deals rather than current market prices. Key differences:

  • Values are higher due to control premium and synergy expectations
  • Data may be less current (deals are historical)
  • Less information is often publicly available versus public company filings
  • Must carefully adjust for market conditions at the time of each deal

Football Field Chart

The football field overlays all three methodologies to show the implied valuation range. Bankers use this to advise clients on a fair range of value and to frame negotiation anchors in M&A discussions.

Key Concepts to Know

  • LTM (Last Twelve Months): Trailing performance; calculated as most recent fiscal year + YTD current year − YTD prior year
  • Calendarization: Adjusting financials so all comps use the same fiscal year-end for apples-to-apples comparison
  • NTM (Next Twelve Months): Forward-looking estimates; generally trades at a discount to LTM (growth priced in)
  • Street consensus: Analyst estimates from Bloomberg, FactSet, or Capital IQ used for NTM projections
