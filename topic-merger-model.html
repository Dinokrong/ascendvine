Merger Model & M&A Concepts

A merger model analyzes the financial impact of one company acquiring another — specifically whether the deal is accretive or dilutive to the acquirer's earnings per share (EPS) and what the combined entity looks like.

Accretion / Dilution Analysis

The central question of a merger model: does the acquisition increase or decrease the acquirer's EPS?

  • Accretive: Combined EPS > Standalone EPS (good for the acquirer)
  • Dilutive: Combined EPS < Standalone EPS (acquirer's shareholders are worse off in the short term)
A deal can be dilutive in Year 1 but accretive in Year 2+ as synergies are realized. Bankers typically analyze accretion/dilution over a 3-year period.

Key Driver of Accretion/Dilution

If the acquirer's P/E multiple is higher than the target's P/E (on an acquisition basis including premium), the deal is accretive in a stock-for-stock deal. If the deal is funded with debt, the comparison is between the after-tax cost of debt and the earnings yield of the target.

Purchase Price & Sources of Funds

The acquirer must decide how to pay for the deal: cash (funded by existing cash or new debt), stock (new shares issued), or a mix of both.

  • All-cash deal: Acquirer bears all execution risk; target shareholders get certainty; can be tax-free if structured correctly
  • All-stock deal: Target shareholders share in upside/downside; dilutes acquirer's share count
  • Mixed consideration: Most common; balances the two

Purchase Price Allocation (PPA)

When an acquisition closes, the purchase price must be allocated to assets and liabilities at fair market value:

  • Write up assets to fair value (creates additional D&A / amortization)
  • Create deferred tax liabilities on asset write-ups
  • Goodwill = Purchase Price − Fair Value of Net Assets

Synergies

Synergies are the incremental value created by combining two companies. They are a key justification for paying a premium.

  • Revenue Synergies: Cross-selling, new markets, combined product offerings (harder to achieve, often discounted)
  • Cost Synergies: Headcount reduction, duplicate overhead elimination, procurement savings (more reliable, usually credited at 50–75% in early diligence)

Merger Model Steps

  1. Project standalone financials for acquirer and target
  2. Determine offer price (usually a premium to target's current share price)
  3. Determine financing mix (cash / stock / debt)
  4. Build sources & uses table
  5. Run purchase price allocation; calculate incremental D&A from write-ups
  6. Combine income statements; add synergies and one-time transaction costs
  7. Calculate pro forma EPS and compare to acquirer standalone EPS
