DCF Analysis

A Discounted Cash Flow analysis values a company by discounting its projected future free cash flows back to the present using an appropriate discount rate. It is the primary intrinsic valuation methodology in investment banking.

The DCF Framework

  1. Project Unlevered Free Cash Flows (FCFs) for a discrete period (typically 5–10 years)
  2. Calculate the Terminal Value (TV) at the end of the projection period
  3. Discount both the FCFs and TV back to the present using WACC
  4. Sum to get Enterprise Value; subtract net debt to get Equity Value

Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF)

FCFF = EBIT × (1 − Tax Rate) + D&A − CapEx − Change in Net Working Capital

Also written as: NOPAT + D&A − CapEx − ΔNWC

FCFF is unlevered (before interest/debt payments), which is why we discount at WACC to get Enterprise Value, then subtract net debt to reach Equity Value.

WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital)

WACC = (E/V) × Ke + (D/V) × Kd × (1 − t)

  • E/V, D/V: Equity and debt as a proportion of total capital (use market values)
  • Ke (Cost of Equity): Calculated using CAPM: Ke = Rf + β × (Rm − Rf)
  • Kd (Cost of Debt): Yield on the company's debt, tax-affected because interest is deductible

CAPM Components

  • Rf (Risk-free rate): Yield on 10-year US Treasury
  • β (Beta): Measures systematic risk relative to the market; unlevered beta strips out capital structure effects
  • Rm − Rf (Equity Risk Premium): Typically 5–7%

Terminal Value

Terminal Value captures all value beyond the discrete projection period — typically 70–80% of total DCF value.

Two Methods

  • Gordon Growth Model (Perpetuity Growth): TV = FCFn+1 / (WACC − g), where g is the long-term growth rate (typically GDP growth, 2–3%)
  • Exit Multiple Method: TV = Final Year EBITDA × EV/EBITDA exit multiple (typically based on comps)
The Terminal Value is extremely sensitive to WACC and growth rate assumptions. Interviewers often ask how TV changes with a 0.5% shift in either — always be ready to discuss the sensitivity.

Sensitivity Analysis

After building the DCF, create a sensitivity table varying WACC (x-axis) and terminal growth rate or exit multiple (y-axis) to show the implied EV range. This is called a "WACC / growth rate sensitivity table" and is always included in a DCF presentation.

DCF Weaknesses

  • Highly sensitive to assumptions — small changes in WACC or growth rate can dramatically change value
  • Terminal value often dominates the result (70–80% of value)
  • Projections are uncertain, especially beyond 5 years
  • Less useful for companies with negative FCF, cyclical businesses, or early-stage companies
