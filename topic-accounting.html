Accounting & the Three Financial Statements

The three financial statements — Income Statement, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow Statement — are the backbone of every financial analysis. Understanding how they link together is the most fundamental skill tested in IB interviews.

The Income Statement

The Income Statement (P&L) shows a company's revenues and expenses over a period of time, resulting in net income.

Key Line Items (Top to Bottom)

  • Revenue — total sales recognized during the period
  • COGS — direct costs of producing goods/services; Revenue − COGS = Gross Profit
  • Gross Profit — the profit after direct costs
  • Operating Expenses (SG&A, R&D) — overhead costs
  • EBIT (Operating Income) — Gross Profit − OpEx
  • EBITDA = EBIT + D&A (commonly used proxy for operating cash generation)
  • Interest Expense / Income — cost of debt or return on cash
  • Pre-Tax Income (EBT) — EBIT − Net Interest
  • Tax Expense — EBT × effective tax rate
  • Net Income — the "bottom line"

The Balance Sheet

The Balance Sheet is a snapshot of what the company owns (assets) and what it owes (liabilities + equity) at a single point in time. Assets always equal Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity.

Assets

  • Current Assets: Cash, Accounts Receivable, Inventory, Prepaid Expenses
  • Non-Current Assets: PP&E (net), Goodwill, Intangibles, Long-Term Investments

Liabilities

  • Current Liabilities: Accounts Payable, Accrued Expenses, Short-Term Debt, Deferred Revenue
  • Non-Current Liabilities: Long-Term Debt, Deferred Tax Liabilities, Pension Obligations

Shareholders' Equity

  • Common Stock + APIC + Retained Earnings − Treasury Stock + AOCI
  • Retained Earnings changes each period by: Net Income − Dividends

The Cash Flow Statement

The CFS reconciles net income to actual cash movement. It has three sections:

Cash from Operations (CFO)

Starts with Net Income, adds back non-cash items (D&A, SBC), and adjusts for working capital changes (ΔAR, ΔInventory, ΔAP).

Cash from Investing (CFI)

Includes capital expenditures (negative), asset sales (positive), and acquisitions (negative).

Cash from Financing (CFF)

Includes debt issuances and repayments, equity issuances, share buybacks, and dividends paid.

Key formula: Ending Cash = Beginning Cash + CFO + CFI + CFF. The ending cash balance on the CFS must match the cash line on the Balance Sheet.

How the Three Statements Link

  • Net Income flows from the Income Statement to the CFS (top of CFO) and to Retained Earnings on the Balance Sheet
  • D&A on the IS is added back on the CFS; accumulated depreciation reduces PP&E on the Balance Sheet
  • CapEx on the CFS increases PP&E on the Balance Sheet
  • Working capital changes on the CFS correspond to changes in current assets/liabilities on the Balance Sheet
  • Net change in cash on the CFS equals the change in the cash account on the Balance Sheet

Key Concepts to Know Cold

  • Accrual vs. Cash Accounting: Accrual recognizes revenue/expenses when earned/incurred, not when cash changes hands
  • Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities (use operating NWC: exclude cash and debt)
  • EBITDA limitations: Ignores CapEx, changes in working capital, interest, and taxes
  • Deferred Tax Assets/Liabilities: Arise from timing differences between GAAP and tax reporting
  • Goodwill: Purchase price − Fair Value of Net Assets in an acquisition; tested annually for impairment, not amortized under US GAAP
