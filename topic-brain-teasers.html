Finance & General Brain Teasers

Brain teasers test your quantitative reasoning, mental math, and ability to think on your feet. The answer matters less than demonstrating a clear, structured thinking process — always think out loud.

How to Approach Brain Teasers

  • Always think out loud. Interviewers want to see your process, not just your answer.
  • Break the problem down. Large estimates = sub-problems multiplied together.
  • Use round numbers. Precision is not the goal — speed and logic are.
  • Sanity-check your answer. Does it make real-world sense?
  • If you get stuck, say so and try a different approach. Transparency is valued.

Finance Brain Teasers — Common Types

Impact of a Change on EPS

Example: "If depreciation increases by $10 and the tax rate is 25%, what happens to EPS?"

Pre-tax income falls by $10 → taxes fall by $2.50 → net income falls by $7.50. EPS = ΔNI / shares outstanding.

Walk Me Through the Statements

Example: "A company buys $100 of equipment with cash. Walk through the 3 statements."

  • IS: No immediate impact (no depreciation yet in that period)
  • CFS: Investing outflow of −$100
  • BS: Cash −$100, PP&E +$100; net assets unchanged; equity unchanged

Percentage Relationships

If revenue grows 20% but margins stay the same, EBITDA also grows 20%. If margins expand, EBITDA grows faster than revenue.

Mental Math Tips

  • 10% rule: Find 10% of a number first, then scale (5% = half of 10%)
  • Factoring: $18 × $6 = $18 × $6 = ($20 − $2) × $6 = $120 − $12 = $108
  • Percentages: 15% of $240 = 10% ($24) + 5% ($12) = $36
  • Fractions: Know common fraction–percent equivalents (1/3 ≈ 33%, 1/6 ≈ 17%)

General Brain Teasers — Estimation (Fermi Questions)

Example: "How many gas stations are in the US?"

~330M people. ~2–3 people per car = ~120M cars. Cars refuel weekly = ~17M fueling events/day. Each station serves ~1,000 cars/day → ~17,000 stations. (Actual: ~150,000 — scale your assumptions accordingly.)

Fermi estimation is about demonstrating a logical decomposition framework, not hitting the exact number. Build your estimate from first principles and defend your assumptions.
