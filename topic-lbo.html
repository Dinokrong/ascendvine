Leveraged Buyouts (LBOs)

An LBO is an acquisition where a financial sponsor (private equity firm) acquires a company primarily using debt, with the target's own assets and cash flows serving as collateral. Returns are driven by debt paydown, EBITDA growth, and multiple expansion.

LBO Overview

In an LBO, the private equity firm contributes ~30–40% of the purchase price as equity, and finances the rest with debt (senior secured, mezzanine, etc.). After a holding period (typically 3–7 years), the sponsor exits via IPO, sale to a strategic, or secondary buyout.

PE firms target IRR of 20%+ (or a 2–3x cash-on-cash multiple) over the holding period. IRR is the primary return metric.

Ideal LBO Candidate Characteristics

  • Stable, predictable cash flows — needed to service debt; low cyclicality preferred
  • Strong free cash flow conversion — low CapEx relative to EBITDA
  • Defensible market position / strong brand
  • Low existing leverage — room to add debt
  • Clear operational improvement opportunities (cost cuts, margin improvement, add-ons)
  • Multiple expansion potential — entering at a lower multiple and exiting at a higher one
  • Tangible assets that can serve as collateral

Sources of LBO Returns

  1. Debt Paydown (Deleveraging): As the company generates cash and repays debt, equity value grows; if EV is flat, equity value = EV − remaining debt
  2. EBITDA Growth: Revenue growth and margin expansion increase the company's exit EV
  3. Multiple Expansion: Exiting at a higher EV/EBITDA multiple than the entry multiple

LBO Model Mechanics

  1. Determine purchase price (entry EV = Entry Multiple × EBITDA)
  2. Build sources & uses — debt tranches, equity contribution, fees
  3. Project operating financials (revenue, EBITDA, CapEx, changes in NWC)
  4. Build debt schedule — model interest, mandatory amortization, cash sweep
  5. Calculate free cash flow available to pay down debt each year
  6. Determine exit value (exit multiple × exit EBITDA)
  7. Calculate equity proceeds = Exit EV − Remaining Debt
  8. Calculate IRR and MOIC (equity proceeds / equity invested)

Debt Tranches

  • Revolving Credit Facility (Revolver): Drawn as needed; lowest cost; first to be repaid
  • Term Loan A (TLA): Amortizing bank debt
  • Term Loan B (TLB): Institutional; minimal amortization (1%/year), repaid at maturity; most common in LBOs
  • Senior Secured Notes: Bonds with hard assets as collateral
  • Mezzanine / Subordinated Debt: Higher yield, lower seniority; sometimes includes warrants
  • Preferred Equity: Hybrid; fixed return, senior to common equity

Key Metrics

  • Entry/Exit Multiple: Purchase price or exit price / EBITDA
  • Leverage Multiple: Total Debt / EBITDA (typically 4–7x in LBOs)
  • IRR: Internal rate of return on the equity investment
  • MOIC / Cash-on-Cash: Total equity proceeds / equity invested (target: 2–3x in 5 years)
  • Interest Coverage: EBITDA / Interest Expense (lenders want >2–3x)
